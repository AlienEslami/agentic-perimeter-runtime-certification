2. FLOAT BARRIERS AND THE PAGE-5 GAP

\FloatBarrier tells LaTeX to place all earlier pending figures and tables
before continuing past that point. A large pending table can therefore
leave an unfinished text page.

The preceding reduced source had three barriers:

  OLD line 263: immediately after the notation table and before Section 2.
    This caused the large blank area on page 5: the notation table needed
    its own page, and the barrier stopped Section 2 from filling the space.
    REMOVED in the revised reduced source.

  OLD line 1795 -> CURRENT line 1858: end of Section 5, Numerical Evaluation,
    immediately before Section 6, Operational Insights and Implementation.
    RETAINED to keep experimental tables ahead of the discussion.

  OLD line 1868 -> CURRENT line 1931: after the conclusion and future work,
    immediately before the author declarations.
    RETAINED to keep any remaining figures/tables out of the declarations
    and appendix. It does not force a page break when no float is pending.

There are now TWO \FloatBarrier commands in main_reduced.tex.

At main_reduced.tex:225, the notation table's placement changed from [!t]
to [!tp]. The added p permits a float-only page. This matters because simply
removing the barrier could let the large table drift far beyond the introduction.
The final layout has Section 2 continuing on page 5 and the notation table
on page 6. The large page-5 gap is filled without changing font size, margins,
the notation entries, equations or introduction wording. The author/layout
revision was 43 pages; the subsequent caption explanations produce 45 pages.

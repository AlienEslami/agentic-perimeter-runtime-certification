YOUR COMMENTS

1. Feasible versus stable.
Replaced "physically and operationally feasible traffic control" in the
abstract with "traffic control that satisfies physical limits and service
requirements". The present results guarantee satisfaction of the stated
constraints over the checked time interval. They do not establish convergence
to an equilibrium, or a Lyapunov stability property, for every accepted plan.
Calling every accepted plan stable would therefore add an unsupported claim.
"Robustly feasible" remains in the mathematical definition in Section 2:
there it precisely means that a plan satisfies the constraints for every
allowed initial state and arrival error. This is different from stability.

2. Expiry wording.
Removed "through its expiry" and the technical terminal-conditions sentence
from the abstract. The replacement says the current plan continues under its
existing guarantee and that conditions are established for maintaining the
guarantees beyond a plan's specified duration. The introduction uses "the
period covered by its certificate". The conclusion follows the same wording.
The mathematical horizon E and the terminal-policy conditions are retained.

3. Tool use.
The introduction now says agents "use tools to develop and revise control
plans". This directly describes the intended activity.

4. Feasibility over the uncertainty family.
Replaced the abstract wording about an undecided family by an explicit
explanation in the introduction: a plan satisfying constraints in simulated
cases may violate them for other allowed initial states or arrival patterns.
Passing selected simulations does not prove the universal guarantee.

5. Proposals changing during execution.
Now says "new plans proposed while the current plan is being executed".
An executing plan is not silently altered. A new candidate is checked before
it can replace the current plan.

6. "Its supervisor".
Removed that premature reference. The rule-based supervisory agent is first
introduced by its role, then called the supervisor.

7. Explicit research gap.
Added a direct, scoped gap: the perimeter-control studies reviewed do not
provide a framework jointly checking agent-proposed plans against boundary
storage and fixed service deadlines under uncertainty while preserving the
guarantees through replacement, rejection and delay. This describes the
combination studied here without claiming that all previous perimeter-control
research lacks guarantees. For example, Zhong et al. establish stability and
robustness for their controllers:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0191261517307671
The gap sentence is a synthesis of the reviewed works, not an exhaustive
claim of priority over every publication.

8. Dual-agent explanation.
Now reads: "An AI agent uses planning tools to propose control plans, and a
rule-based supervisory agent checks them before execution." The following
sentence names this arrangement as the dual-agent architecture.

9. Maintaining the plan and certificate.
Removed this record-management detail from the introduction. The supervisor
now checks whether a proposal can replace the current plan while meeting
the physical and service requirements. The decision record in Section 3
still stores the current plan and its original certificate because rejection
must preserve the already established guarantee.

10. Introduction example.
Removed the Region 2 priority / Region 1 deadline example and the accompanying
feedback sentence. The architecture description is shorter and more direct.

11. Activation uncertainty.
Yes: this means the time the proposed plan actually starts. The introduction
now says "state-estimation errors at the time a plan starts and errors in
arrival forecasts". The later explanation includes propagation of uncertainty
while the current plan executes during computation.

12. Why plans expire.
Section 3.2 now explains that a plan specifies control inputs on [t0,E], and
its certificate checks those inputs under the stated model, uncertainty and
requirements on that same interval. E ends the checked period. It is not a
claim that traffic becomes unsafe immediately after E. To continue the
guarantee, a certified replacement must be ready by E, or a terminal policy
must already have been certified for the state reached and pending service
requirements. A finite list of control blocks alone supplies neither future
inputs nor a proof for all future time.

13. "Feasibility checks".
Replaced this wording in the introduction and roadmap with the certification
procedure or explicit checks of physical constraints and service requirements.
Section 4 is now titled "Runtime Certification Using Traffic Structure".
The formal feasible-plan set in Section 2 is unchanged.

14-15. Technical approach detail in the introduction.
Removed the admission-cancellation sentence and the detailed list of
coordinates, nonnegativity and signed uncertainty propagation. Those details
remain in Section 4, where their symbols and mathematical role are explained.

16. Paragraph flow.
Combined the overlapping problem, architecture and approach paragraphs.
The progression is prior work -> practical risks -> assurance methods ->
specific gap -> proposed architecture -> guarantee scope -> contributions.

17. First contribution.
Rewritten around bounding regional vehicle counts and boundary queues.
It explains that conservation and relationships between uncertain traffic
quantities tighten the checks, and mentions the extension to several queues.
The former list of specialized mathematical terms is removed.

18. Second contribution.
Rewritten around successive replacements, computation delays, and continued
execution after rejection, under the stated traffic and interface assumptions.
Conditional continuation beyond the planned period remains, without an
appendix-location announcement in the contribution itself.

19. Third contribution.
Shortened to a repair search between a proposal and a certified backup,
minimum control-input change among the tested certified alternatives, and
retention when no replacement is ready. It does not claim global optimality,
guaranteed computation time, or unconditional indefinite continuation.
The analytical repair-cost result is preserved in Appendix F.

CLAUDE'S COMMENTS

C1. Explicit gap: addressed as described in item 7.

C2. Corollary 1: removed the separate hypothesis requiring every accepted
replacement to contain the activation state and cover pending requirements.
The proof now derives state containment from valid external information and
propagation under the current plan (or a valid estimate at activation), then
uses Theorem 1 / Proposition 1 for certification and Algorithm 1's activation
rule for pending requirements and matching execution data. Algorithm steps 3
and 4 now identify those constructions explicitly. The proof then invokes
Theorem 2. Trusted inputs, interface integrity, the initial certificate,
finitely many decisions, and coverage until the next decision or final time
remain necessary hypotheses. An unnumbered containment inclusion is added
to make the argument visible. This is a clearer derivation from existing
results, not a new stability theorem or a weakened uncertainty assumption.

C3. Shared uncertainty: corrected the three listed remnants and two more
in the delayed-activation discussion and Appendix B. The revised wording
refers to dependence between state errors or past and future states, rather
than suggesting that a statistical correlation model is required.

C4. Organization and consistency:
- Moved the terminal-continuation and repair-cost pointers from Section 4.6
  to the opening of Section 4.
- Moved the finite-precision qualification immediately after Lemma 1's proof.
- Standardized "service requirement", "certification procedure", and
  "execution interval" in the displayed text.
- Changed the sentence beginning with w to "The arrival error w ...".
- Moved the descriptive service qualifier after the execution inequality
  to prevent a line overrun. The inequality and its scope are unchanged.

C5. Fixed repository reference:
The bibliography and repository links now point to release paper-2026-11-10,
with a neutral Reproduction Guide title. The release includes the manuscript
and the saved November comparison evidence in addition to the existing
reproduction sources. Repository visibility remains private.

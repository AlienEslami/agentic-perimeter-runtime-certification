ASSESSMENT

The Age operator was present before the notation revision. It advances the
active priority and suspended-task stack against stored absolute expiries.
It does not measure vehicle age or add a new traffic-state variable.

The notation review found no algebraic error caused by the symbol changes.
One units issue needed an explicit convention: task instructions use
integer minutes, whereas the traffic equations use hours. Task creation
and extension now divide their minute durations by 60 in both papers.
The saved implementation uses the equivalent integer-minute clock.

A mathematical result can be stated in words. Its classification depends
on its logical role and significance. Theorem 2 and Theorem 4 state the
sequential-execution and continuation guarantees; Proposition 2 supplies
the repair-installation guarantee; Corollary 1 combines ordered repair
with the terminal result. Their types and numbers are retained. Their
statements and proofs now show the mathematical objects and conditions
used to reach those guarantees.

WHAT WAS ADDED

1. Task aging and expiry

   The first unexpired task is selected from the active task followed by
   the suspended tasks in restoration order. An empty survivor set gives
   network operation. The text proves

       Age_t(Age_s(theta)) = Age_t(theta),  t >= s,

   on the active task and suspension stack. The case s=t gives
   idempotence. Audit-history timestamps are excluded from this identity.
   Original expiries are preserved when tasks are suspended or restored.

   A second invariant gives expiry as creation time plus the original
   duration and all extensions, with minutes converted to hours. An
   induction over task operations proves the invariant. The planner
   interface also specifies the invalid-request case: no proposal is
   generated and the execution layer retains a valid incumbent.

2. Theorem 2 - successive replacement or retention

   A trajectory family is attached to each stored certificate. A displayed
   update rule selects the replacement family on acceptance and retains
   the incumbent family on rejection. Retention keeps the original
   correlated uncertainty history rather than restarting its proof.

   Explicit premises require the actual installation state to lie in the
   certified initial set, the next decision to occur before expiry, and
   every pending admitted service row to remain covered. The proof gives
   an induction invariant for each executed trajectory segment and then
   derives the physical inequalities and deadline inequalities.

   Rejection preserves guarantees while the incumbent contract remains
   valid. A newly imposed hard obligation must be covered by the retained
   or installed certificate before admission. Changing a soft objective
   does not alter the admitted hard obligations.

3. Proposition 2 - repair installation and computation timeout

   A piecewise rule selects a repair only when its certificate is ready
   by the actual installation time and passes for that installation
   record. Otherwise the incumbent continues. The installed repair's
   initial set must contain the actual state. Pending service rows must
   be covered by the selected record, and time/model/command coefficients
   must match its certificate.

   The proof concatenates the certified incumbent prefix with the repair
   suffix, or retains the same incumbent trajectory family. A late result
   cannot change a command already executed. Retention preserves the
   original expiry.

4. Theorem 4 - continuation after finite-plan expiry

   A terminal checker combines the finite physical/service checks,
   pending-service coverage, implementable controller transition and
   terminal reachable-set inclusion in the certified invariant set K.
   The theorem gives the execution policy explicitly: use the active
   finite plan before expiry and the backup policy afterward.

   The proof shows that a plan reaching expiry lands in K, that robust
   invariance covers the following backup interval, and that locally
   finite installations allow these certified segments to concatenate
   for all future time. The displayed conclusion includes the physical
   constraints at every future time and every admitted service deadline.
   Requests during backup require a new covering terminal certificate.
   These are conditional guarantees; the simulations remain finite-plan
   studies and were not changed to claim an indefinite-operation test.

5. Corollary 1 - terminally certified repair grid

   The certified subset of the repair grid is defined explicitly. The
   endpoint P(1)=P^B belongs to it under the stated incumbent terminal
   certificate, so the set is nonempty. Selected repairs land in K;
   retaining the incumbent on timeout preserves its terminal connection.
   Theorem 4 then yields continued physical and service feasibility.
